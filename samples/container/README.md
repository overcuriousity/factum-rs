# Sample evidence container

`sample.aff4l` is an AFF4-L v1.0 evidence container written by factum, for checking whether other AFF4-L tools
(Magnet AXIOM, pyaff4, your own) read it and accept its signatures — without real case data. It holds three small
files: two added, the container closed and signed, reopened, a third added, and closed and signed again, so it
carries two closed states (`factum/history/1/` and `factum/history/2/`).

Both closes are signed twice:

- **GPG**: `information.turtle.asc`, by the key in `gpg-public.asc`
  (`factum sample (TEST ONLY) <sample@factum.invalid>`, fingerprint `A6C24358FDE38819288B7DA39BD2726059D99421`);
- **X.509**: `information.turtle.factum.sig` (CMS, made with `gpgsm`) with its chain in
  `information.turtle.factum.cer`, issued by the root in `root.pem` (`CN=factum sample root (TEST ONLY)`).

**Every key here is a throwaway, made for this sample only, and marked TEST ONLY.** Never trust them for anything
else. The private keys were deleted after signing.

## Verifying it

- In factum: **Verify Container…** (trust the root in gpgsm first, or the X.509 check fails as untrusted).
- Without factum (Python 3, `gpg`, `openssl`):

  ```
  unzip -j sample.aff4l verify_container.py
  python3 verify_container.py sample.aff4l --ca root.pem --use-carried-key
  ```

- In another tool: open `sample.aff4l` and check that it lists the three files and accepts the signatures. The
  standard leaves the PEM structure of X.509 signatures open; factum writes detached CMS. Reports of what other
  tools make of it are welcome as issues.

## How it was made

`scripts/sample-container.sh` makes the keys (gpg, openssl, gpgsm) and runs `examples/sample_container.rs`, which
builds the container with factum's library. It is regenerated by hand when the container format changes; CI checks
that the committed sample still verifies.
